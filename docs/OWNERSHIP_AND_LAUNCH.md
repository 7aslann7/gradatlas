# GradAtlas ownership, attribution and sustainability memo

Checked 12 October 2026. This is general source-based research and a launch proposal, not a determination of the founder's legal title or a negotiated legal agreement.

GradAtlas can be open source, remain free for students, retain a named founder and official service, and receive sponsorship or commercial offers. Publishing does not itself give away copyright ownership. It does give other people the permissions stated in the chosen licence. GitHub distinguishes ownership of submitted content from the licences needed to host and publicly fork it. A copyright licence and an assignment of ownership are different transactions. [GitHub Terms, D.3–D.6](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service), [WIPO: Copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)

## What the current MIT licence means

The current `LICENSE` notice is `Copyright (c) 2026 GradAtlas contributors`. The MIT licence has been retained.

MIT allows copying, modification, distribution, sublicensing and sale of copies, including commercial reuse. A recipient can make a competing or proprietary fork; MIT does not require that recipient to open its modifications. Copies or substantial portions must retain the copyright and permission notices. This is useful legal attribution, but MIT does not require a prominent founder badge, backlink or website footer. It is therefore insufficient to promise that every downstream user will visibly credit the founder on their public homepage. [OSI: MIT licence](https://opensource.org/license/MIT)

Changing a repository to public is distinct from granting a general open-source licence. GitHub permits public viewing/forking through its functionality; additional reuse permissions come from the chosen licence. [GitHub: Licensing a repository](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository)

Do not plan to erase previously issued permissions by deleting MIT, making the repository private, selling it, or changing future releases. Already released MIT copies can continue circulating under their existing terms. MIT's text does not expressly use the word “irrevocable”; the precise enforceability of a grant is jurisdiction-dependent, so avoid claiming an absolute result in every country. GitHub's legal guide distinguishes future licensing from treatment of existing contributions, and an OSI-hosted licence-review discussion specifically explains the persistence of permission on already released code. The latter is community analysis, not a court ruling. [GitHub's legal guide](https://opensource.guide/legal/), [OSI licence-review discussion, August 2025](https://lists.opensource.org/pipermail/license-review_lists.opensource.org/2025-August/005866.html)

## Founder credit, copyright ownership and project control

Use an explicit, accurate “Founded and maintained by [preferred founder name]” line in the README and official site's About/footer, supported by release history and contribution records. Keep the official repository and domain linked consistently. This documents origin and makes recognition practical; it does not exclude lawful forks.

The current collective copyright notice does not personally identify the founder. A future notice could name the verified founder/rightsholder alongside other contributors, but a name in a notice cannot manufacture ownership. Retain upstream and contributor notices. Project administration, founder status and ownership of every code contribution are separate things.

Protectable works generally receive copyright automatically; registration is not what creates authorship. Turkish official guidance says that having a software idea or paying for its development alone does not establish software authorship. Actual author, employer, contractor and assignment arrangements matter. [WIPO: Copyright protection](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/protection), [Türkiye Ministry of Culture: General questions](https://telifhaklari.ktb.gov.tr/TR-332449/genel-sorular.html)

Contributors generally retain rights in their own protectable contributions, subject to employment and other arrangements. GitHub's default is that contributions are licensed under the repository licence; that is not a copyright assignment. A DCO certifies a right to submit under the indicated licence and does not transfer ownership. A CLA can grant additional permissions, while an assignment is a separate, explicit transfer. Do not claim a generic CLA automatically makes the founder sole owner. [GitHub Terms, D.6](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service), [Developer Certificate of Origin](https://developercertificate.org/), [WIPO: Assignment versus licensing](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo_pub_909_2016.pdf)

For a student-focused MIT project, a clear inbound-MIT contribution policy and optional DCO are proportionate proposals. If future exclusive transfer or additional licensing rights are essential, obtain appropriately drafted contributor agreements before relying on those rights. Advice is needed at that decision point, rather than treating current founder credit as sufficient legal title.

## Brand and data are separate

The GradAtlas name, official domain and branding are distinct from the code licence. MIT contains no express trademark grant. A clear trademark/brand policy can identify the official project and prohibit misleading endorsement claims, subject to applicable law; it must not silently take away the MIT permissions for code.

Using the name, owning a domain or adding a policy is not trademark registration. Türkiye's Industrial Property Code Article 7 states that its trademark protection is obtained through registration. WIPO notes that some countries also recognize use-based rights. Check name availability in the intended markets before claiming exclusive brand rights; consider registration as adoption grows. No availability search or registration was performed here. [TÜRKPATENT-hosted Code, Article 7](https://webim.turkpatent.gov.tr/file/ff5f9aba-c277-4022-8486-9b11e525c765?download=), [WIPO: Trademark protection](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks/protection)

Programme facts differ from copied descriptions, photographs, logos, maps and database collections. Copyright can protect expression or original selection/arrangement rather than facts themselves; database-specific extraction rights may additionally apply in relevant jurisdictions. Do not assume publicly accessible university pages are public-domain content, or that placing a root MIT file can license rights GradAtlas never acquired. Keep original short summaries, factual provenance links and per-asset terms. [WIPO: Facts and databases](https://www.wipo.int/export/sites/www/sme/en/documents/guides/customization/creative_expression_pa.pdf), [EU Database Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=celex%3A31996L0009)

The repository already has `docs/DATA_POLICY.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, and now identifies ROR/GeoNames and ExcelJS licence scopes. It does not establish a complete rights-transfer inventory. Clarify the scope of the code licence and decide an appropriate licence for GradAtlas-owned dataset content separately. Preserve MapLibre, Natural Earth and font terms. This is a proposal; no dataset licence was selected or altered.

## A free product can have sustainable funding

GitHub Sponsors currently supports eligible recipients in Turkey, subject to its onboarding and payment conditions. It supports one-time and monthly sponsorships. GitHub currently charges no platform fee for personal-account sponsorships and up to 6% for organization-account sponsorships; this does not settle a recipient's tax or other financial obligations. [GitHub: About Sponsors](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/getting-started-with-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors)

Suggested plan, without a premium student tier:

1. Public launch: name the founder and maintainers, explain the free-access mission, publish source provenance/correction rules, and distinguish the official service from forks.
2. Funding: invite voluntary sponsorships for hosting, accessibility, translations and verified-data maintenance. Publish a concise budget and sponsor acknowledgments; keep programme placement and factual claims independent from sponsorship.
3. Partnerships: discuss grants, university outreach, infrastructure sponsorship or paid organizational support/implementation where useful. Such optional services do not need to restrict the public student product.
4. Commercial-interest readiness: maintain a simple inventory of code rightsholders, contributor terms, dependencies, dataset rights, domains, branding and service contracts. Track real use and correction quality; do not imply that open sourcing guarantees investment.

## What can be sold or acquired later

An open-source project can be part of a commercial transaction. Depending on actual ownership and contract terms, assets could include a company, official brand/domain, operated service, contracts, permitted dataset rights, and the founder's assignable economic rights. The team and maintenance capability can also motivate an offer. This is a business inference, not an assurance that GradAtlas has a buyer or a particular valuation. [WIPO: IP assignments](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/technology-transfer/agreements)

A buyer cannot be promised exclusive control over every existing MIT copy. The founder also cannot assign other contributors' rights merely by transferring the GitHub repository. An acquisition can transfer the founder's actual rights and official service assets while documenting existing public licences and preserving the free service by contractual commitments. Before accepting an exclusive-rights deal, have the title chain, contributor agreements, existing licence grants and relevant Turkish formalities reviewed by a qualified professional. A decision published through Türkiye's official judicial service discusses FSEK Article 52's requirement for written arrangements identifying the relevant economic rights separately; a repository handover is not a substitute for the proper transaction documents. [Official judicial decision discussing Article 52](https://mevzuat.adalet.gov.tr/ictihat/1089588600)

Recommended current direction: keep MIT only if the founder accepts commercial reuse and forks; make founder attribution explicit, establish proportionate contribution terms, clarify brand/data scope, and fund the free public product through voluntary sponsorship and transparent partnerships. No licence file was changed in this research.
